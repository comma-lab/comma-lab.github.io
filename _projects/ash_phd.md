Jordie Shier's work on [percussive timbre remapping](/projects/jordie_phd/) contributes novel machine learning techniques and tools for working with timbre in NIME contexts; this technical development is grounded in longitudinal co-design with two percussionists, each reported as an annotated portfolio, confronting the gap between a working demo and a mapping a musician will keep performing with.

[Noel-Hirst, Saitis, and Bryan-Kinns (2025)](https://zenodo.org/records/16946825) turn the same critical and co-design lens on generative AI (GenAI), drawing on the established but understudied practice of sample-based music to ask how a technology increasingly built on recycling existing sonic/timbral material can support meaningful creativity, and translating an ethnography of two London communities ([CreateDefineRelease](https://www.createdefinerelease.com/) and [ClubWIP](https://www.clubwip.co/)) into design directions for generative AI tools. 

We found that context was extremely important for making sample-based music, and identified four aspects of sampling context relevant to GenAI: _environment_, _community and identity_, _reality and fiction_, and _agency_. Subsequent [work presented at the **Second International Conference in AI Music Studies (AIMS 2026)**](https://www.noelhirst.net/research/Is%20depth%20real/index/#context-in-sample-based-music-communities) argued that generative AI systems currently prioritise content over context and are thus ill-equipped to deal with the values and practices of sample-based music making. 

Next we want to better understand the role semantically structured generative AI might play in sample-based music making. We created a tool ([MALT](https://www.noelhirst.net/design/malt/index/)) for dynamically creating latent controls for generative models based on arbitrary attributes, such as timbre descriptors and raw audio features. By asking sample-based musicians to make music with MALT, we want to understand (i) if semantically mediatiated models are helpful for sample-based music making, (ii) what they miss, and (iii) how the four themes of context in sample-based communities are affected by control interfaces with different levels of semantic mediation.
